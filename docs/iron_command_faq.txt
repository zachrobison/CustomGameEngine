IRON COMMAND — FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (DIRECT ANSWERS)
==========================================================

This file answers common questions about Iron Command directly. Each question
and answer is self-contained so it can be retrieved and quoted on its own. Facts
match the game's rules and recipes.


--- THE BASICS ---

Q: What is Iron Command?
A: Iron Command is a first-person factory-and-strategy game. You build an
automated factory that turns ore into robots and defenses, then use that army to
destroy your rivals. It blends factory-building (like Satisfactory) with
real-time strategy combat (like Command & Conquer).

Q: What is the theme of Iron Command?
A: A cheesy 1950s atomic-age science-fiction theme — ray guns, chrome robots,
and domed bases.

Q: Is Iron Command single-player or multiplayer?
A: It is designed as a multiplayer free-for-all. Every player fights every other
player; there are no teams.

Q: What view is the game played from?
A: A first-person view. You walk around your factory on foot, build, and fight
with a ray gun, and you command robots from a separate top-down tactical map.


--- WINNING AND LOSING ---

Q: How do you win Iron Command?
A: You win by being the last competitor standing. When every rival's home base
(the Hub) is destroyed and yours is still alive, you win.

Q: How do you lose Iron Command?
A: You are eliminated when your own Hub is destroyed. That is the only way to
lose the match.

Q: What happens when your character's health reaches zero?
A: You respawn next to your Hub at full health and keep playing. Dying does not
lose the match — only losing your Hub does. The player is a commander, not the
objective.

Q: Can you lose even if your army is strong?
A: Yes. If your Hub is destroyed you are out of the match no matter how large
your army is. Protecting the Hub is the top priority.


--- THE HUB ---

Q: What is the Hub?
A: The Hub is your home base. It is the thing you must protect; if it is
destroyed you lose. It is also your command center.

Q: How much health does the Hub have?
A: The Hub has 1400 health.

Q: Do you build the Hub yourself?
A: No. The Hub is premade — it is automatically placed at the spot you choose
when you drop onto the map, and it is kept clear of ore deposits. You spawn
standing next to it.

Q: What does the Hub do besides being a base?
A: Standing next to the Hub and pressing the interact key opens the top-down
Command Map, where you command robots and draw your defenses.


--- RESOURCES AND ITEMS ---

Q: What are the item types in Iron Command?
A: There are seven: Ore, Ingot, Screw, Plate, Rod, Part, and Robot. They go in
roughly increasing order of refinement.

Q: Where does everything come from?
A: Everything starts from Ore, which is mined from ore deposits and refined
through a chain of machines into finished robots and defenses.

Q: What is a Part used for?
A: Part is an advanced component and also the upgrade currency in the game.

Q: How much ore does each item cost?
A: Cumulative raw-ore cost: Ore = 1, Ingot = 1, Screw = 0.5, Plate = 1, Rod = 1,
Part = 1.5 (from Rod+Screw) or 2 (from Plate+2 Screws), Robot = 4, Turret = 2,
Mine = 2, Fence = 2, Tripwire = 1.5.

Q: What is the most expensive item?
A: The Robot, at 4 ore (2 Plates + 4 Screws, which is 4 ingots total).

Q: What is the cheapest defense?
A: The Tripwire, at 1.5 ore. Turrets, Mines, and Fences each cost 2 ore.


--- MACHINES AND RECIPES ---

Q: What machines (buildings) are there?
A: Miner, Smelter, Constructor, Assembler, Barracks, Terminal, Hub, and the
Conveyor belt.

Q: What does the Miner do?
A: The Miner must be placed on an ore deposit and mines ore. It has three speed
settings: Mine Ore (1 ore per 1.4 s), Overclock (2 ore per 1.4 s), and Turbo
(3 ore per 1.2 s).

Q: What does the Smelter do?
A: It converts 1 Ore into 1 Ingot, taking 2 seconds.

Q: What does the Constructor do?
A: It turns ingots into intermediate parts: 1 Ingot into 2 Screws, or 1 Ingot
into 1 Plate, or 1 Ingot into 1 Rod.

Q: What does the Assembler do?
A: The Assembler is the key machine. It makes Parts, Robots, and all defenses:
Part (1 Plate + 2 Screws, or 1 Rod + 1 Screw), Robot (2 Plates + 4 Screws),
Turret (1 Plate + 1 Rod), Mine (1 Plate + 2 Screws), Fence (2 Rods), and
Tripwire (1 Rod + 1 Screw).

Q: What does the Barracks do?
A: The Barracks is a storage depot. Robots are belted into it and held until you
order them out from a Terminal or the Hub Command Map.

Q: What does the Terminal do?
A: The Terminal has no production. Interacting with it opens the Command Map, the
same top-down tactical view the Hub provides.

Q: What does the Conveyor belt do?
A: Conveyor belts automatically carry items from one machine to another,
splitting and merging the flow of items between machines.

Q: How do you build a Robot and what does it cost?
A: Robots are made at the Assembler from 2 Plates + 4 Screws, taking 4 seconds
each. That is 4 ore (4 ingots) per robot.

Q: How fast can one Assembler make Robots?
A: One Assembler on the Robot recipe makes 1 robot every 4 seconds, which is
15 robots per minute at full feed.


--- DEFENSES ---

Q: What defenses can you build?
A: Fences, Mines, Turrets, and Tripwires. They are manufactured at the Assembler.

Q: How do you place defenses?
A: Defenses are not placed directly in the world. They are first made at an
Assembler, where they pile up in a stockpile, and then you draw them onto the
map from the Hub Command Map.

Q: What does a Fence do?
A: A Fence is a tall wall (like a tall conveyor belt stood on end) that
physically blocks robots from passing. You drag to lay a wall of fences.

Q: What does a Mine do?
A: A Mine detonates when any hostile robot (an enemy's, never your own) steps
near it, dealing heavy area damage. You paint a minefield by dragging a box on
the Command Map.

Q: What does a Turret do?
A: A Turret is an automated gun that fires on nearby enemies. You place turret
clusters by dragging a box on the Command Map.


--- COMBAT AND COMMANDING ---

Q: What weapon does the player have?
A: A ray gun. It fires an instant hitscan beam that damages hostile things it
hits, such as enemy robots and rival Hubs.

Q: How do robots fight?
A: Robots move and shoot at the same time without stopping. They automatically
seek and fire on the nearest enemy in range, or march on the nearest rival Hub
if no enemies are near.

Q: How do you command your robots?
A: Open the Command Map at your Hub or a Terminal, choose the March tool, and
click or drag to send all your robots to that spot. You can order them into
unexplored territory.

Q: What is the Command Map?
A: A flat top-down tactical view opened at the Hub or a Terminal. It has four
tools: March (send robots), Fence, Mines, and Turrets (draw to place defenses).

Q: Is there a minimap?
A: Yes. A live minimap in the top-right corner shows your Hub and robots and,
updating continuously, every rival's Hub, robots, and player position.


--- MULTIPLAYER ---

Q: Are there AI enemies in Iron Command?
A: No. There are no AI opponents. The only enemies are the other human players.

Q: How do players connect for multiplayer?
A: One player clicks "Host a match," and the game shows that player's local
network address. The other players choose "Join" and type in that address.

Q: What network port does Iron Command use?
A: It uses port 49813.

Q: Do players need to be on the same network?
A: Yes. Standard play requires all players to be on the same local network (the
same Wi-Fi or router). Playing across the internet is not built in directly; it
needs an external tool such as a virtual-network app, router port-forwarding, or
a relay server.

Q: Can you see other players in the match?
A: Yes. Rivals appear in your world — their Hub, their robots, and their player
avatar — and update live. Both players must finish dropping into the battlefield
before they can see each other.


--- CONTROLS ---

Q: What are the controls?
A: WASD moves your character; the mouse looks and aims; left mouse button fires
the ray gun or places a building; B toggles Build mode; right mouse button
dismantles a building in Build mode; E interacts (opens a machine's recipe menu,
the Hub or Terminal Command Map, or the lobby War Table); and ESC opens the
Pause menu.

Q: How do you pause the game?
A: Press ESC. The Pause menu (Resume, Main Menu, Quit) freezes the game and
frees the mouse cursor.

Q: How do you enter Build mode?
A: Press B. In Build mode you place machines and draw conveyor belts; the right
mouse button dismantles the targeted building.


--- FACTORY STRATEGY ---

Q: How do you balance a Robot production line?
A: To keep one Assembler making Robots at full speed, use roughly: 1 Assembler,
1 Constructor on Plate, 1 Constructor on Screws, 2 Smelters, and 1 Overclock
Miner (or about two basic Miners). That produces 15 robots per minute.

Q: What is usually the first bottleneck in a factory?
A: Mining. Switch Miners to Overclock or Turbo, or add more Miners on more ore
deposits, before scaling up the rest of the chain.

Q: What is the cheapest way to make Parts?
A: Make Parts from a Rod + Screw (1.5 ore) instead of a Plate + 2 Screws (2 ore).
It is about 25% cheaper in ore.

Q: How do you make a bigger army?
A: Duplicate the whole Robot line. Each full line adds 15 robots per minute and
needs its own Assembler, Plate Constructor, Screw Constructor, 2 Smelters, and
about 1 Overclock Miner.
