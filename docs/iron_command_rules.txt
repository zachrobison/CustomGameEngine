IRON COMMAND — GAME RULES AND MECHANICS
========================================

This document describes the rules, systems, and controls of Iron Command, a
first-person factory-and-combat strategy game. It is written as reference
material: each section is self-contained so it can be read on its own.


1. OVERVIEW
-----------
Iron Command is a first-person "factory real-time-strategy" game with a cheesy
1950s atomic-age science-fiction theme (ray guns, chrome robots, domed bases).
It blends two genres: building an automated factory (in the style of
Satisfactory) and commanding an army to destroy your rivals (in the style of
Command & Conquer).

You play as a commander. You land on a battlefield, build a factory that
automatically turns raw ore into robots and defenses, and use that army to
destroy every rival's home base. It is a free-for-all: there are no teams, and
the last commander with a surviving home base wins.

The game is played from a first-person view. You walk around your factory on
foot, place buildings, and fight with a ray gun, while also commanding robots
from a top-down tactical map.


2. HOW TO WIN AND LOSE
----------------------
- WIN CONDITION: You win by being the last competitor standing. When every
  rival commander's home base (the "Hub") has been destroyed and yours is still
  alive, you win the match.
- LOSE CONDITION: You are knocked out of the match when your own Hub is
  destroyed. Losing your Hub is the only way to be eliminated.
- PLAYER DEATH IS NOT A LOSS: If your character's health reaches zero in combat,
  you do not lose. You respawn next to your Hub at full health and keep playing.
  Only the Hub being destroyed ends your match. The player is a commander, not
  the objective.


3. THE HUB (YOUR HOME BASE)
---------------------------
The Hub is your home base and the thing you must protect. It is the heart of
the game's win/lose rules.

- The Hub is PREMADE: it is automatically placed at the spot you choose when you
  drop onto the map. You do not build it yourself, and it is always nudged clear
  of resource (ore) deposits so it never blocks your mining.
- You spawn standing next to your Hub at the start of a match.
- The Hub has a large pool of health (1400 points). Enemy robots and enemy
  players attack it; when its health hits zero, it is destroyed and you are out.
- The Hub is also your command center: when you stand next to it and press the
  interact key, it opens the top-down Command Map (see Section 8).


4. GAME FLOW: FROM MENU TO BATTLE
---------------------------------
A match proceeds through several phases:

1. MODE SELECT: Choose Offline (skirmish) or a LAN multiplayer option (Host a
   match, or Join a match by entering the host's network address).
2. LOBBY: A walkable staging room. Walk to the glowing "War Table" in the center
   and interact with it to proceed to deployment.
3. DROP: A top-down map of the battlefield appears. Click where you want to land.
   Your premade Hub is placed there and you spawn beside it.
4. PLAY: The main game. Build your factory, defend your Hub, and attack rivals.


5. THE BATTLEFIELD
------------------
The battlefield is a large, procedurally generated outdoor map (roughly 740
units across). It contains:
- ORE DEPOSITS (resource nodes): the raw material your whole economy starts
  from. Miners must be placed directly on these.
- FORESTS and HILLS: trees and raised terrain that act as obstacles and cover.
- PASSAGEWAYS: cleared corridors through the forests.
- FOG OF WAR: the map starts unexplored. You reveal terrain by walking around.
  You can still command robots into unexplored areas from the Command Map.


6. THE PRODUCTION CHAIN (RESOURCES AND ITEMS)
---------------------------------------------
Everything is built from ore through a chain of refining steps. The seven item
types are:
- ORE: raw material mined from deposits.
- INGOT: smelted from ore.
- SCREW, PLATE, ROD: intermediate components made from ingots.
- PART: an advanced component; Parts are also the currency used for upgrades.
- ROBOT: the finished combat unit.

Items are carried automatically between machines by conveyor belts.


7. BUILDINGS (MACHINES)
-----------------------
You enter Build mode to place machines on the ground. Each machine is selected
from the build menu, placed on a grid, and then given a recipe by interacting
with it. The building types are:

- MINER: Must be placed on an ore deposit. Mines ore. It has three selectable
  recipes that trade speed for output: "Mine Ore" (1 ore), "Overclock" (2 ore),
  and "Turbo" (3 ore).
- SMELTER: Converts 1 Ore into 1 Ingot (about 2 seconds).
- CONSTRUCTOR: Converts ingots into intermediate parts. Recipes:
  1 Ingot -> 2 Screws; 1 Ingot -> 1 Plate; 1 Ingot -> 1 Rod.
- ASSEMBLER: The most important machine. It makes Parts, Robots, and all the
  defenses. Recipes:
    * 1 Plate + 2 Screws -> 1 Part
    * 1 Rod + 1 Screw -> 1 Part
    * 2 Plates + 4 Screws -> 1 Robot
    * Turret (from 1 Plate + 1 Rod)
    * Mine (from 1 Plate + 2 Screws)
    * Fence (from 2 Rods)
    * Tripwire (from 1 Rod + 1 Screw)
- BARRACKS: A storage depot for robots. Robot items are belted in and held here
  until you order them out from a Terminal or the Hub Command Map.
- TERMINAL: Has no production. Interacting with it opens the Command Map, the
  same top-down view the Hub provides.
- HUB: Your home base (premade, not built by hand — see Section 3).
- CONVEYOR BELT: Carries items from one machine to another. Belts are drawn as a
  path and automatically split and merge the flow of items between machines.


8. THE COMMAND MAP (COMMANDING YOUR ARMY)
-----------------------------------------
Press the interact key next to your Hub (or at any Terminal) to open the Command
Map: a flat, top-down tactical view of the battlefield. From here you command
your robots and place your defenses by drawing on the map. The map has a tool
palette with four tools:

- MARCH: Click or drag anywhere to send all of your robots to that spot. You can
  order them into unexplored territory, not just places you have already seen.
- FENCE: Drag to lay a wall of fences. A fence is a tall barrier (like a tall
  conveyor belt stood on end) that physically blocks robots from passing.
- MINES: Drag a box to paint a minefield — a grid of mines fills the area.
- TURRETS: Drag a box to place a cluster of automated turrets.

Fences, mines, and turrets are not placed directly in the world. They are first
manufactured at an Assembler, where they accumulate in a stockpile ("bank").
You then spend that stockpile by drawing them onto the Command Map from the Hub.


9. COMBAT
---------
- YOUR RAY GUN: Your character carries a ray gun. Firing it is a hitscan (instant
  beam). It damages anything hostile it hits — enemy robots and rival Hubs.
- ROBOTS: Your robots move and shoot at the same time without stopping. They have
  an aggression zone: they automatically seek out and fire on the nearest enemy
  within range, or march on the nearest rival Hub if no enemies are near.
- DEFENSES:
    * FENCE: a wall that blocks enemy robots from advancing.
    * MINE: detonates when any hostile robot (an enemy's, never your own) steps
      near it, dealing heavy area damage.
    * TURRET: an automated gun that fires on nearby enemies.
    * TRIPWIRE: a trap that triggers on a passing enemy.
- MINIMAP: A live minimap in the top-right corner always shows your Hub, your
  robots, and — updating continuously — every rival's Hub, robots, and player
  position.


10. MULTIPLAYER (FREE-FOR-ALL)
------------------------------
Iron Command is designed as a free-for-all: every player is independent and
fights everyone else.

- There are NO AI opponents. The only enemies are the other human players. (In a
  match with no other players, there is simply no one to fight.)
- Each player has their own Hub, their own factory, and their own robots.
- You can see rivals in your world: their Hub, their robots, and their player
  avatar all appear and update live.
- CONNECTING: One player clicks "Host a match" and the game shows that player's
  local network (LAN) address. The other players choose "Join" and type in that
  address. Everyone plays over the same local network. The network port used is
  49813.
- SAME-NETWORK REQUIREMENT: Standard play requires all players to be on the same
  local network (the same Wi-Fi/router). Playing across the internet is not
  built in directly; it requires an external tool (such as a virtual-network app
  like Tailscale, router port-forwarding, or a relay server).
- Both players must finish dropping into the battlefield before they can see
  each other.


11. CONTROLS (DEFAULT)
----------------------
- WASD: move your character.
- MOUSE: look around and aim.
- LEFT MOUSE BUTTON: fire the ray gun (or place a building while in Build mode).
- B: toggle Build mode (place machines and draw conveyor belts).
- RIGHT MOUSE BUTTON: dismantle the targeted building (while in Build mode).
- E (interact): open a machine's recipe menu, open the Hub/Terminal Command Map,
  or use the War Table in the lobby.
- ESC: open the Pause menu (Resume / Main Menu / Quit). Pausing freezes the game
  and frees the mouse cursor.


12. STRATEGY NOTES
------------------
- Your economy is the foundation: ore -> ingots -> components -> robots/defenses.
  A bigger, faster factory means a bigger army.
- Parts double as upgrade currency, so advanced production also funds upgrades.
- Defenses (fences, mines, turrets) are made in the factory first, then drawn
  onto the Command Map from your Hub — plan production for defense, not just
  offense.
- Protect the Hub at all costs: losing it ends your match, no matter how strong
  your army is.
- Dying is only a setback — you respawn at your Hub — so you can play
  aggressively with your ray gun.

14. GLOSSARY
------------
- HUB: your home base; its destruction eliminates you; last Hub standing wins.
- COMMANDER: the player; a commander is not the objective and respawns on death.
- COMMAND MAP: the top-down tactical screen opened at the Hub or a Terminal, used
  to march robots and draw defenses.
- MINER / SMELTER / CONSTRUCTOR / ASSEMBLER / BARRACKS / TERMINAL: the factory
  machine types.
- CONVEYOR BELT: automatic item transport between machines.
- ORE / INGOT / SCREW / PLATE / ROD / PART / ROBOT: the seven item types, in
  roughly increasing order of refinement.
- PART: advanced component and the upgrade currency.
- ROBOT: the mobile combat unit you mass-produce.
- DEFENSES: fences (walls), mines (area traps), turrets (auto-guns), tripwires.
- BANK / STOCKPILE: the reserve of manufactured fences/mines/turrets waiting to
  be drawn onto the map.
- FOG OF WAR: unexplored terrain, revealed by walking.
- FREE-FOR-ALL: every player fights every other player; no teams.
- LAN: local area network; the default way multiplayer connects.


15. QUICK FACT SUMMARY (FOR RETRIEVAL)
--------------------------------------
- Genre: first-person factory real-time strategy. Theme: 1950s atomic-age sci-fi.
- Objective: destroy all rival Hubs; be the last Hub standing.
- You lose only if your own Hub is destroyed.
- Player death causes a respawn at the Hub, not a loss.
- Economy chain: Ore -> Ingot -> Screw/Plate/Rod -> Part -> Robot.
- Machines: Miner, Smelter, Constructor, Assembler, Barracks, Terminal, Hub,
  Conveyor belt.
- Defenses: Fence (wall), Mine (area trap), Turret (auto-gun), Tripwire.
- Defenses are built in the Assembler, stockpiled, then drawn onto the Command

- Weapon: a hitscan ray gun that damages enemy robots and rival Hubs.
- Multiplayer: LAN free-for-all, no AI enemies, connect by host IP on port 49813.
- Hub health: 1400. Map size: about 740 units across.
